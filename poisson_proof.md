# Is the Unknown-Episode Count Poisson? (Proof + Verification)

**Claim.** Conditional on the built layers, the number of unknown episodes
N(T) in a run of length T (or in any mileage window) converges to a
Poisson distribution in the rare-event limit p → 0, where p is the
per-transition probability of opening an episode (p ≈ 0.004 here). At
p = 0.004 the exact deviation from Poisson is of order 0.2–0.3% in the
dispersion index — statistically invisible. The proof is standard renewal
theory (Rényi's thinning theorem) plus superposition; the empirical
section below confirms it on the simulator.

## Step 1 — one layer: episode starts are a thinned renewal process

Fix a layer with mean sojourn μ and Gamma(κ, θ) durations (κ = μ²/σ²,
θ = σ²/μ, so κθ = μ). Its transitions occur at renewal epochs of the
renewal process with i.i.d. inter-renewal times X ~ Gamma(κ, θ). Because
the next element is drawn i.i.d. from the layer's fixed transition vector
q (independent of the current element), each transition opens an episode
with probability

    p = m − s,   m = Σ_{u∈U} q_u   (unknown mass),   s = Σ_{u∈U} q_u²,

where s removes self-hops onto the same unknown element (rule 4). The
waiting time between consecutive episode starts is therefore a geometric
sum of renewal intervals:

    W = X_1 + … + X_G,   G ~ Geometric(p) on {1, 2, …},

and the starts form a **renewal process** themselves (i.i.d. W's).

**Exponential limit (Rényi 1956).** The Laplace transform of W is

    E[e^{−λW}] = p·φ(λ) / (1 − (1−p)·φ(λ)),   φ(λ) = (1 + θλ)^{−κ}.

Rescale time by the episode rate r = p/μ, i.e. evaluate at λ·p/μ. Since
φ(λp/μ) = 1 − κ·(θ/μ)·λp + O(p²) = 1 − λp + O(p²) (using κθ = μ),

    E[e^{−λ p W / μ}] = p(1 − λp + O(p²)) / (p + λp + O(p²))
                      → 1 / (1 + λ)   as p → 0,

which is the Laplace transform of Exp(1). So the normalized inter-start
times rW converge in distribution to Exp(1); a renewal process with
exponential inter-arrivals **is** a Poisson process, hence the layer's
episode starts converge to a Poisson process of rate r = (m − s)/μ. ∎

*(Technical footnote: consecutive open-indicators are not fully
independent — indicators at transitions i and i+1 share the element E_i,
a 1-dependent structure. The Chen–Stein Poisson approximation theorem
covers exactly this local dependence; the induced total-variation error is
O(p), i.e. of the same negligible order as the corrections already shown.
With self-transitions disabled the indicators are exactly i.i.d.)*

## Step 2 — superposition over layers

The four unknown-bearing layers are mutually independent processes (no
conditional probabilities between layers; separate elements, vectors, and
sojourn draws). The superposition of independent point processes that each
converge to Poisson converges to a Poisson process with the summed rate
(a special case of Grigelionis' superposition theorem):

    Λ_total = Σ_k (m_k − s_k)/μ_k    per second.

Hence the total count in any interval — a full run of T seconds or a
mileage window of w miles — converges to Poisson(Λ_total·T) resp.
Poisson(Λ_total · w·3600/mph). Counts of disjoint windows become
independent in the same limit.

## Step 3 — how far from Poisson at p = 0.004? (exact correction)

For a renewal counting process the index of dispersion converges to the
squared coefficient of variation of the inter-arrival time. For the
geometric sum W:

    E[W] = μ/p,   Var[W] = Var(X)/p + E[X]²(1−p)/p²,
    CV²(W) = 1 − p·(1 − CV²(X)) = 1 − p·(1 − 1/κ).

So each layer's episode-start stream has asymptotic dispersion
1 − p(1 − 1/κ), slightly **below** 1 because Gamma sojourns with κ > 1
are more regular than exponential ones. With p ≈ 0.004 and κ ∈ [1.8, 4]
across our layers, the deviation is between 0.18% and 0.30% — over an
order of magnitude smaller than the sampling noise of a dispersion
estimate from 200 windows (sd ≈ √(2/199) ≈ 0.10). In other words: not
exactly Poisson, provably Poisson-in-the-limit, and indistinguishable
from Poisson at any realistic sample size.

## What is NOT Poisson

- **Unconditionally across construction seeds** (element counts, rarity
  shuffles, Dirichlet draws), N is a *mixed* Poisson: Var[N] =
  E[Λ]T + T²·Var[Λ] > E[N]. Overdispersion across differently-built
  models is expected and real — the Poisson claim is conditional on one
  built model, which is the relevant case for a fixed simulator
  configuration. (At concentration_scale = 20,000 the mixing variance is
  small; at the old c = 100 it would have dominated.)
- The **old per-tuple encounter counting** (v1) was strongly clustered
  (dispersion ≈ 12): bursts of new tuples during one unknown occupancy
  violate the rare-independent-increments structure. The episode
  semantics is precisely what restores the Poisson property.

## Empirical verification (generated by poisson_tests.py)

**A. Replication experiment.** 300 independent runs of 3,000 miles over the *same* built layers (construction seeds fixed, run-time seeds varied). Under the Poisson hypothesis the run counts are iid Poisson.
- counts: mean 58.64, variance 63.22, dispersion 1.078; dispersion test (R-1)·D = 322.3 vs chi2_{299} -> p = 0.338
- chi-square GOF vs Poisson(58.6): stat 34.8 on 27 dof -> p = 0.144

**B. Window counts of the 2M-mile run** (200 disjoint 10,000-mile windows):
- mean 196.13, variance 187.41, dispersion 0.956; test statistic 190.2 vs chi2_199 -> p = 0.677

**C. Inter-arrival distances of the 2M-mile run** (n = 39,227) vs Exponential(mean 50.98 mi): KS statistic 0.00553 -> p = 0.182 (exponential gaps <=> Poisson process)

**Verdict:** all tests PASS at the 5% level - the episode counts are statistically indistinguishable from Poisson, exactly as the theory predicts for p ~= 0.004.
